# Rubin Ultra 4-Die-Class Packaging: A LATC Process Route

## Executive position

Industry reporting has indicated that an originally discussed four-compute-die Rubin Ultra-class package encountered manufacturability constraints at extreme package scale, with warpage repeatedly identified as a central packaging concern. NVIDIA has not publicly confirmed that warpage was the reason for any product-architecture change. Cools therefore presents the four-die case as an engineering application scenario based on reported industry information, not as a statement of NVIDIA's confidential process history.

The technical point remains decisive: a package carrying four near-reticle-scale GPU compute dies and a large HBM4E population creates a bonding area at which conventional full-package heating and post-solidification correction become increasingly ineffective. Once the solder joints solidify under a warped geometry, the deformation and joint-height nonuniformity become locked into the package.

## Why LATC is directly relevant

Cools Large Area Thermal Clutch (LATC) changes the bonding sequence rather than merely strengthening a cooling unit.

1. A monolithic lid or rigidizing member holds the package in a planar state.
2. Energy is delivered selectively to the bonding interface instead of heating the full package mass.
3. Solder is allowed to solidify while planar constraint is maintained.
4. The thermal path is immediately switched to high-speed conductive cooling.

This sequence targets the specific failure mechanism that emerges at four-die package scale: multidirectional curvature, local contact loss, solder-joint height deviation, and residual-stress locking during solidification.

## Strategic implication

LATC provides a process route for restoring the single-package four-die architecture that conventional large-area bonding may not manufacture reliably. The value is not limited to warpage reduction. It may enable:

- recovery of the original four-die integration objective;
- reduction of local non-contact and joint-height dispersion;
- faster solidification and cooldown under constraint;
- expansion of practical package area without accepting the same warpage penalty; and
- an alternative advanced-packaging route for foundries, system companies, and late-moving competitors.

## Cools patent position

The application is supported by Cools patent families directed to pre-solidification coupling of a rigidizing member, wavelength-selective heating from the substrate underside, integrated bonding-sequence control, and adjacent thermal-path-switching and pressurized conductive-cooling architectures.

## Core message

> LATC is not a method for flattening a package after warpage has formed. It is a manufacturing architecture for preventing warpage from being locked into an extreme-scale four-die package during solder solidification.
